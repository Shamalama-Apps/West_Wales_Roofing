# Legal details the website needs

Written 3 September 2026. This is a checklist to work through, not legal advice.
Confirm anything uncertain with an accountant or solicitor before launch.

---

## 1. Must appear on the website

**The trading disclosure.** This is the one most sole traders miss.
"West Wales Roofing" is not Will's own surname, so it is a *business name* under
the Companies Act 2006. That means the website must show:

- Will's full name as the owner
- an address where legal documents can be served on him

It is not enough to have this on invoices. Websites are explicitly covered.

**If it is a limited company instead**, the requirement is different and stricter:

- the registered company name exactly as at Companies House
- the company registration number
- the registered office address
- the part of the UK it is registered in (England and Wales, or Wales)

**Contact details.** A geographic address, an email address, and the phone number.
Email and phone are already on the site; the address is not.

**VAT number**, if he registers. Not required below the threshold, and a brand new
firm almost certainly is not registered yet. If he does register, the number must
appear on the site.

## 2. Must be given to the customer, not published on the site

**The right to cancel.** This is the one with real teeth. When a roofer agrees a
job at the customer's home, that is an *off-premises contract*, so the customer
gets **14 days to cancel**, and Will must give them **written notice of that right**
before work starts.

Getting this wrong is not a technicality:

- failing to give the notice is a criminal offence
- the cancellation window extends from 14 days to **12 months**
- a customer can cancel a finished job and Will may be unable to charge for it

If the customer wants work started inside the 14 days, they must ask for that in
writing, and even then they can still cancel and pay only for work already done.

A one-page cancellation notice attached to every quote covers it. Worth getting
a template from Trading Standards or an accountant rather than writing one.

## 3. Must exist in the business, and worth showing on the site

- **Public liability insurance.** Not legally required, but the single thing
  homeowners look for hardest, and most will not book without it. Show the cover
  amount and insurer.
- **Employers' liability insurance.** Legally required as soon as he employs
  anyone, even one person part time, and the certificate must be available to
  staff. Fines are per day without it.
- **Waste carrier registration.** A roofer taking away stripped slate, felt or
  tiles is transporting waste, which requires registration with **Natural
  Resources Wales**. Often overlooked; it is free or low cost.
- **Trade bodies and certifications** (NFRC, CompetentRoofer, TrustMark). Optional,
  and only show what he actually holds. Claiming membership he does not have is a
  misleading commercial practice and an offence in itself.

## 4. Already handled

- **Privacy policy** is written, live at `/privacy/`, and honest. It needs the
  business identity from section 1 adding to the "Who we are" section.
- **Cookie banner not needed.** The site sets no cookies and uses no analytics or
  tracking. Nothing to consent to. Worth keeping it that way.

## 5. Worth having, not required

- **Terms and conditions**: payment terms, deposits, what a quote includes and
  excludes, what happens if the roof turns out worse once stripped.
- **A written guarantee**, with the length and what it covers. If the site claims
  one, it has to be real and honest.

## What I need in order to update the site

1. Sole trader or limited company?
2. If a company: registered name, number, and registered office address.
3. If a sole trader: Will's full name, and an address for service.
4. Public liability cover amount and insurer.
5. Any trade body memberships he actually holds.
6. Guarantee length on new roofs, if he offers one.

Items 1 to 3 are needed before launch. The rest can follow, but 4 is the one that
wins work.
